What is being simulated

The can is a thin aluminium shell: 0.1 mm side wall, thicker neck and base, a stiff double seam and a 5182-alloy lid, about 3,000–6,000 nodes. Each step computes in-plane stretching (von Mises plasticity with hardening) and bending (curvature from every edge's fold angle, with the fully plastic moment σyt²/4). The crusher and bed are rigid steel with friction, and folds push on each other through self-contact.

Reading the curve

  • Start force is the first peak, where the wall buckles. That is what your crusher must overcome.
  • Average force × travel is the energy. A lever cuts the force but not the energy.
  • The steep rise at the end is the folds stacking solid. Stop the stroke before it.

Accuracy

Checked against plate-bending theory (within 1–9%), ring crush theory (elastic within 2%, plastic collapse within 8%), the classical buckling load of the bare wall (within 10%), and the ~1–1.5 kN axial load can makers design to. Fold patterns are chaotic, so expect ±10–15% between runs, just as with real cans. Change the random can # to see the spread.
